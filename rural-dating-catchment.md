# Rural Dating Catchment (RDC)

## Purpose
RDC evaluates whether a rural/exurban property has practical access to a substantial suburban dating population while preserving acreage and development opportunity. It is a location-access model, not a judgment about individual residents.

## Standard cohort
Use women age **30–39** as the standardized Census comparison cohort because ACS tables cleanly provide 30–34 and 35–39 bands. Separate never-married from divorced/separated when data permit. “Unmarried” is a demographic proxy, not proof that someone is actively dating.

Race/ethnicity may be reported only as descriptive demographic context. **Never exclude or discount people by race/ethnicity or use race as a proxy for attractiveness, fitness, personality, or lifestyle.** Model desired characteristics directly with appropriate lifestyle/activity/geographic proxies.

## Geography
Do not use the whole MSA as the primary dating pool. Separate:
1. **Urban core** — exclude from the primary RDC count when not realistically relevant to the rural property.
2. **Suburban dating belt** — primary population of interest.
3. **Rural property belt/interface** — where acreage and development opportunities become realistic.

Prefer actual drive-time catchments over straight-line distance. Traffic and road-network friction matter.

## Core outputs
- **RDC-30** — estimated relevant unmarried women 30–39 within ~30 minutes.
- **RDC-45** — estimated relevant unmarried women 30–39 within ~45 minutes. Primary comparison band.
- **RDC-60** — estimated relevant unmarried women 30–39 within ~60 minutes.
- **RDC confidence** — LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH.
- **Suburban Pool Depth** — SMALL / MEDIUM / LARGE / VERY LARGE / ENORMOUS.
- **Rural Interface** — POOR / FAIR / GOOD / EXCELLENT / EXCEPTIONAL.
- **Date Friction** — LOW / LOW-MODERATE / MODERATE / HIGH.
- **Growth Direction** — whether population/development pressure is moving toward the subject property/corridor.

When using ACS small-area estimates, retain margins of error and avoid false precision. Rounded ranges are preferable when sampling uncertainty is material.

## Lifestyle context
Use education/professional population, growth/transplant churn, outdoor recreation, physical-activity/fitness indicators, suburban/exurban character, social venues and app practicality only as probabilistic context. Census data do not measure attractiveness, fitness, compatibility, or active dating status.

## RDC × Development intersection
Explicitly assess whether the property is simultaneously close to a substantial suburban dating belt, genuinely rural enough for acreage/shop/homestead goals, in or near the metro's path of development, and positioned for future subdivision/resale demand.

## Discovery corridors — initial working set
These are hypotheses to test, not permanent winners:
- Charlotte N/NW: Lake Norman / Mooresville → Troutman / Statesville / Lincolnton / Rowan fringe.
- Raleigh: outer Triangle, especially Johnston/Franklin/Granville interfaces.
- Nashville N: Goodlettsville / White House → Robertson County.
- Nashville SE: Murfreesboro → Christiana / Rutherford fringe.
- Columbus N: Polaris / Westerville / Lewis Center → Delaware / Sunbury / outer Delaware County.
- Indianapolis N/SE: Westfield/Noblesville → outer north; Greenwood/Franklin → Shelby County.
- Cincinnati NE: Mason / West Chester / Loveland → Lebanon / Wilmington / Clermont fringe.
- Kansas City: Johnson County/Northland → selected Cass/Miami/Platte exurbs.
- Atlanta N/NW: Alpharetta/Roswell/Woodstock → Canton/Ball Ground/Cartersville; evaluate corridor-specific traffic.
- DFW: retain for corridor testing because of exceptional suburban scale despite potential traffic friction.

## Discovery threshold
Prioritize new-property discovery where **Suburban Pool Depth is LARGE or better + Rural Interface is EXCELLENT or better + Date Friction is LOW or LOW-MODERATE**. Corridor-specific evidence can qualify other markets even when metro-wide friction is worse.

Keep Green Bay/Appleton as the baseline/control when useful.
