# Resale and Development

Evaluate the property as a long-term asset and future exit: **Am I buying at a defensible price in a place people are likely to continue wanting, and does the land have optionality that could create value later?**

Do not confuse a cheap property with a good investment. A bargain in a shrinking, illiquid market can be worse than paying more for scarce land in the path of durable demand.

## Required Area Analysis
Evaluate the surrounding **township/municipality, county and nearest meaningful metro**, not just the ZIP code.

Look for evidence of:
- 5- and 10-year population/household direction where available.
- Employment base, major employers and economic diversity.
- Median household income and direction relative to the region/state.
- Housing demand: sale volume, DOM, inventory, price direction and buyer depth when reliable data exists.
- Building permits/new construction, subdivisions and commercial/industrial development.
- Highway/interstate access and realistic commute shed.
- Broadband, sewer/water expansion and other infrastructure investment.
- Major announced employers, plants, hospitals, logistics, data centers, schools or public/private investment.
- Whether nearby metro/suburban growth is moving toward the property.
- Agricultural/recreational/second-home demand where that is the more relevant market.
- Warning signs: persistent population loss, employer concentration/closures, weak transaction volume, long DOM, falling/flat real prices, excess vacant housing, poor infrastructure and very small future buyer pool.

## Development Optionality
Do not assume acreage can be subdivided or developed. Research:
- Zoning and future land-use plan.
- Minimum lot size/frontage.
- Number of plausible additional lots/homesites.
- Road/frontage/access geometry.
- Septic/well feasibility or public utility availability.
- Wetlands/floodplain/topography/soil constraints.
- Easements, deed restrictions and OGM/surface-right issues.
- Utility-extension cost and practicality.

Classify development potential:
**NONE / LIMITED / PLAUSIBLE / STRONG / EXCEPTIONAL**.

Estimate development value only when evidence supports it; do not value hypothetical lots as finished lots without subtracting entitlement, infrastructure, carrying, sales and risk costs.

## Market Trajectory
Give every property a separate **Market Trajectory** rating:

- 🟢 **GROWING** — durable demand/population/employment/infrastructure evidence; appreciation tailwind likely.
- 🟢/🟡 **EMERGING** — credible catalysts or spillover growth, but not yet fully established.
- 🟡 **STABLE** — adequate buyer pool and economy; limited obvious growth tailwind.
- 🟡/🔴 **STAGNANT** — weak growth and/or thin liquidity; resale may take longer.
- 🔴 **DECLINING** — persistent structural decline materially threatens appreciation/liquidity.
- ⚫ **UNKNOWN** — insufficient evidence.

Also report **Resale Liquidity: STRONG / ADEQUATE / THIN / VERY THIN / UNKNOWN** and **Development Optionality: NONE / LIMITED / PLAUSIBLE / STRONG / EXCEPTIONAL**.

## Evaluation Notes
- Penalize overpaying much more severely in stagnant/declining or thinly traded markets.
- Allow a reasonable premium for genuinely scarce acreage in a growing path-of-development market, but verify that the premium is supported by comps and demand.
- Favor properties with multiple future buyer pools: residential, hobby farm, homestead, equestrian, recreation, business, timber/agricultural or development buyers.
- Favor land near expanding metros where rural acreage is becoming scarcer, provided zoning/access/utility constraints do not destroy the optionality.
- Separate broad market appreciation potential from property-specific forced appreciation through improvements or subdivision.
- Improvements should create durable market value, not merely satisfy a highly personal preference.
- Penalize title/OGM/access/easement issues that shrink the future buyer pool or complicate financing/development.
- Compare effective cost after improvements with likely stabilized value rather than judging asking price alone.
- A property can score well for lifestyle and poorly here. Make that conflict explicit.
