# Greenhouse Business

Evaluate the property for a **small greenhouse/nursery business that begins as a hobby or side business and can expand gradually if demand justifies it**.

The property does not need to support a large commercial greenhouse operation on day one. Favor low-cost entry, legal flexibility, good growing conditions, convenient utilities, customer access and room to scale without disrupting the home/property.

## Evaluate

### Legal / Zoning
Research whether the property can legally support:
- Personal/hobby greenhouses.
- Multiple or larger greenhouse structures.
- Plant propagation and nursery activity.
- Home occupation or agricultural business.
- On-site retail/customer pickup.
- Farm stand/roadside sales.
- Seasonal employees if expansion occurs.
- Signage and parking.
- Storage sheds, potting buildings and related accessory structures.

Distinguish a greenhouse allowed as a residential accessory structure from a commercial nursery/retail use. Identify permits, conditional-use requirements and meaningful restrictions.

### Site Quality
Look for:
- Open, sunny, preferably south-facing or otherwise high-solar-exposure areas.
- Relatively level, well-drained ground.
- Low wetland/flood/hydric-soil conflict.
- Reasonable wind exposure/protection.
- Space near water/electric without consuming the property's best homesite or operational land.
- Room for outdoor growing beds, nursery stock, compost, soil/media storage and equipment.

### Water / Utilities
Evaluate:
- Well capacity or public water availability when known.
- Practical irrigation source and expansion potential.
- Electric service near likely greenhouse sites.
- Propane/natural gas/wood/other practical heating options.
- Cost/difficulty of extending utilities to the greenhouse area.

Do not assume an existing residential well can support a materially larger commercial operation without verification.

### Climate / Growing Economics
Consider:
- USDA hardiness/growing environment.
- Frost-free/growing-season length.
- Winter heating burden.
- Summer heat/ventilation burden.
- Whether season extension materially improves the business opportunity.

A colder climate is not automatically bad, but penalize properties where heating cost makes year-round production economically unrealistic unless the likely business model is seasonal.

### Market Access
Identify realistic customer markets within roughly 30/60/90 minutes. Consider:
- Population and household growth.
- Higher-income suburban/exurban customers.
- Farmers markets.
- Garden/landscape demand.
- Farm-stand/direct-sale opportunity.
- Potential wholesale customers such as landscapers, garden centers, restaurants or small farms.
- Visibility/access if on-property sales are contemplated.

Favor properties that can reach a meaningful customer base without requiring the property itself to sit in an expensive commercial corridor.

### Hobby-to-Business Path
Explicitly assess whether the property supports this progression:

1. **HOBBY** — one small greenhouse and personal growing.
2. **SIDE BUSINESS** — plant starts, vegetables/herbs/flowers, seedlings or nursery products sold locally.
3. **SMALL COMMERCIAL** — multiple structures, greater production, customer pickup/farm stand and/or wholesale activity.

Rate the property's **Scale-Up Path: EASY / PLAUSIBLE / DIFFICULT / BLOCKED / UNKNOWN**.

## Scoring Notes

- Do not heavily penalize the absence of an existing greenhouse if a good site exists and construction is legal/economical.
- Existing greenhouse infrastructure is valuable only if condition, placement and utility access are useful.
- Strong zoning freedom and a good sunny building site can outweigh existing structures.
- Penalize restrictive zoning/HOA rules, poor water availability, severe shading, wetlands, difficult access or no practical customer market.
- Give additional credit when greenhouse activity complements other property uses such as homesteading, chickens/livestock, composting, farm goods, firewood/lumber customers or a roadside farm stand.
- Consider whether greenhouse structures and business traffic would interfere with privacy, dogs, residence use or future homesites.
- Avoid assuming commercial viability merely because agricultural use is allowed; customer-facing retail may have separate rules.
