## Development Analysis

### County rules that matter

Washington County AG zoning permits agriculture and planned large-lot residential development. The code sets a **1.5 net-buildable-acre minimum** for individual lots; approved subdivisions may reach **1 acre with public water** or **20,000 sq ft with public sewer + public water**. The subdivision ordinance defines subdivision as division into 3+ lots and treats certain >=1.5-acre public-road-frontage splits as exempt divisions. County review also requires septic soil testing and highway ingress/egress review; new county-accepted roads must meet county standards.

### Development thesis

The parcel's basis is not obviously bad: recent local land sales show roughly **$7.2k/acre** for 73.67 acres (2025), about **$12.6k/acre** for 5.15 acres (2026), and **$14.4k/acre** for 8 acres (2026). But those per-acre figures do **not** establish this property's residual value because the subject has easement-only access, unproven septic, river/flood constraints and unknown utility-extension cost.

The key issue is **access economics, not acreage**. If the recorded easement is only sufficient for one residence/recreational use, the development thesis collapses. If it can be widened/upgraded and legally serve multiple lots, a small estate-lot concept may become plausible.
