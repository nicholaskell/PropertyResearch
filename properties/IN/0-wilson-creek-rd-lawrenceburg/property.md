## Development Analysis
**Development Score: 71/100 (provisional)**

The strongest concept is frontage-driven rural acreage lots rather than a dense internal-road subdivision. Dearborn County defines a Minor Subdivision as up to seven buildable lots plus the parent tract along an existing public street/private drive without a new public street or publicly maintained facility extension. Eight or more buildable lots, or a project requiring a new public street/public-maintenance facilities, is a Major Subdivision.

### Preferred Development Strategy
Pursue **4–7 premium 2–5 acre homesites**, preserving road frontage and avoiding a new internal public road if geometry permits. Confirm septic first. This is likely more capital-efficient and faster than maximizing theoretical 1-acre density.

### Scenario Range
1. **Estate-lot strategy:** ~4–6 lots of ~4–8 acres. Lowest entitlement/infrastructure burden; strongest privacy product; lower total revenue.
2. **Capital-efficient strategy:** ~5–7 lots averaging ~3–5 acres, frontage-dependent. Best fit with minor-subdivision threshold and Cincinnati exurban demand.
3. **Higher-yield strategy:** ~8–15+ lots near 1–2 acres. Theoretical zoning density supports this, but major-subdivision procedures, septic, frontage/internal road, stormwater and infrastructure can make it substantially more expensive and slower.

**Realistic saleable lot yield:** provisionally 5–7 without major internal-road work; higher yield requires geometry, perc and engineering confirmation.
