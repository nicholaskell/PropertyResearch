## Zoning / subdivision findings
### A-2 is favorable, but not yet confirmed for this APN
Switzerland County §153.041 states that A-2 is intended for agricultural areas where residential uses have occurred/are anticipated, with a minimum lot area of one acre, and expressly permits residential subdivisions with large lots. Single-family dwellings and manufactured homes are permitted uses.

A-1 is different: county code is designed to protect prime agricultural land and the zoning-code reclassification procedure indicates an A-1 subdivision may need to demonstrate Group III/IV soils and satisfy paved-road/drainage criteria before the Plan Commission initiates rezoning to A-2. Therefore, **official A-2 confirmation is a closing-level diligence item, not a paperwork detail.**

### Frontage/access constraint
County zoning defines a LOT as a parcel accessible by a street/place and, for residential uses, abutting a street/place for at least 50% of the prescribed district lot width. With only ~330 ft of subject-road frontage, creating multiple conventional independent road-front lots is inherently limited. A compliant internal street/private-place design may unlock rear acreage, but Chapter 152 standards and Plan Commission interpretation must be obtained before assigning value to 4+ lots.

## RV / temporary occupancy
Current county temporary-use code materially worsens the RV thesis:
- RV parking for visitation: maximum seven days under a temporary-use permit framework.
- Temporary mobile-home living quarters are allowed in certain narrow circumstances, including health-care accessory use or security purposes, generally requiring Board approval and time limits.
- A mobile home may be used as a temporary office during construction/development, but that does not authorize ordinary residential occupancy in an RV while building a house.

**Underwriting position:** assume **no legal construction-period RV residence** unless Switzerland County Planning/BZA provides written parcel-specific approval explaining the permitted unit, duration, building-permit prerequisite, septic/wastewater method, water/power and inspections.

## Septic / soils
The Health Department requires a certified soil-scientist report before a new septic permit, followed by system design, county review, site inspection and final inspection. For subdivision underwriting, one successful homesite is insufficient: test the specific conceptual lots. A 3-lot concept should have at least three acceptable primary/reserve septic areas established before treating the lots as buildable inventory.
