## Backup Plan if Subdivision Fails

The reason for subdivision failure determines whether the fallback works.

- If subdivision fails only because Public Works requires uneconomic multi-lot roadway improvements, a single residence on the intact 11 acres may still be legal/valuable if one-home access is accepted and septic/buildability clear.
- If subdivision fails because of defective legal access, title restrictions, inability to establish any septic site, or severe site constraints, the single-house fallback may fail too. Those must remain purchase contingencies.

### Single-House Spec / Acreage-Homestead Exit

A retail-GC spec build is financially fragile because 2026 North Alabama/Madison County builder guidance commonly places straightforward builder-grade+/entry custom construction roughly in the $165–$230+ per heated square-foot range before land and significant site work. A wooded/sloped 11-acre tract also adds clearing, drive, septic, utilities, grading, drainage, design/permits, carrying and selling costs.

Preferred fallback product: ~1,600–1,900 sf simple 3/2 one-story house, slab if soils/topography permit, attached or detached 2-car garage / ~24x30 shop, selective clearing of roughly 1–2 acres, retain the balance wooded. Avoid luxury finishes and excessive square footage.

- Vertical house: ~$250k–$310k if aggressively cost-controlled / owner-managed.
- Site, septic, utilities, drive, clearing: ~$40k–$70k.

- Approx. all-in: ~$445k–$570k.
- Plausible resale target based on acreage-home evidence: roughly $525k–$625k depending product/site/finish/market.

This can produce a reasonable profit if construction is tightly controlled and the finished product earns an acreage premium, but it is not a robust fallback if built through a high-cost retail custom GC. The project should be purchased on the assumption that single-house fallback is acceptable but not relied on for a guaranteed flip profit.
