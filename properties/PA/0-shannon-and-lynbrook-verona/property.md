## Access

The listing claims both parcels are accessed from Dalecrest Drive. Penn Hills requires a highway occupancy/building-permit process for a new single-family driveway and requires driveway design to provide safe movement and conform to municipal construction standards.

For subdivision economics, the critical issue is not merely whether the parent parcel touches Dalecrest. It is whether enough legal/public frontage exists for independent lots or whether a public/private road extension is required. That question is unresolved.

## Zoning & Buildability

The listing reports only **RES**. Exact Penn Hills zoning district remains P0.

If R-1 applies, current ordinance allows single-family residences, agriculture/gardening, accessory uses and farms/farm animals on parcels of 5+ acres. R-1 minimum lot area is 8,400 sq ft, minimum frontage/average width 60 ft, and maximum density 5 DU/acre. Those figures are **not** a realistic lot-yield forecast.

Penn Hills permits only one principal residential structure per zoning lot outside planned development/multifamily districts. Any subdivision must produce lots that meet zoning plus Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance requirements.

A particularly important constraint for Nicholas's former shop-first strategy: **no permanent accessory building may be constructed before the principal structure.**

## Temporary RV Occupancy

**Status: FAIL / written confirmation required.**

The ordinance defines a camper/RV as a residential accessory use when located in a side/rear yard or garage. It does not identify RV occupancy as a principal residential use in R-1/R-1A/R-2, and permanent accessory structures cannot precede the principal structure. Therefore the normal “build pole barn first, park/occupy RV inside while building” sequence should be treated as unavailable unless Penn Hills Planning/Code Enforcement gives a property-specific written path.

Exact validation: Penn Hills Planning, 412-342-1086; ask whether a temporary occupied RV is allowed during active construction on a vacant residential parcel, what permit triggers it, and whether any temporary-use provision overrides the accessory-use rule.

## Animals & Homestead

Penn Hills allows farms and farm animals on parcels of five acres or more in R-1 and related low-density districts. The 12.426-acre parcel clears that threshold if zoned accordingly; the 4.794-acre parcel by itself does not. Chickens have separate special-exception/registration/coop-permit rules. This is suburban homesteading flexibility, not unrestricted rural zoning.
