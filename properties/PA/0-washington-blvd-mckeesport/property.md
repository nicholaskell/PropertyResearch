## Zoning: Important Listing Discrepancy

Current MLS mirrors describe the two frontage lots as residential and the rear ~24 acres as "Agricultural." Liberty Borough's current published zoning ordinance, however, establishes only **Commercial, "A" Residence and Industrial** use districts. It does **not** establish a separate Agricultural district. Within "A" Residence, farms, truck gardens, nurseries and noncommercial greenhouses are permitted.

Therefore, the MLS "Agricultural" label should be treated as **UNVERIFIED shorthand**, possibly describing use rather than the legal zoning district. Obtain a written zoning determination for each PIN.

If the tract is in "A" Residence / First Area District, the code permits one-family dwellings and sets a **6,500 sq ft minimum lot area** for a one-family dwelling, with a 25-ft front yard and side/rear requirements. This theoretical density is not a realistic yield estimate for the rear tract because roads, slopes, drainage, stormwater, utilities and landslide constraints dominate.

## Subdivision / Infrastructure

Liberty Borough requires subdivision plans to go through the Planning Commission and Council. Its subdivision code generally requires new streets to have at least a **50-ft right-of-way and 26-ft paved cartway**. Council may require grading, paving, curbs, water mains, sanitary/storm sewers and streetlights to be installed or financially guaranteed before final approval/sales.

This is the key economic problem. The tract's long narrow geometry means a rear-lot subdivision could require a relatively long internal road before enough saleable frontage is created. On steep/unstable ground, road + retaining + drainage + utility costs can overwhelm the attractive $6k/gross-acre acquisition basis.
