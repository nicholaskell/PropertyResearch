## Land / Development Read

The central issue is not gross acreage but how much of the 12.5 acres remains **dry, accessible, septic-capable and economical to serve**. The creek setting and narrow approach can turn a seemingly inexpensive infill acreage parcel into an expensive development site.

Hamilton County subdivision regulations require residential frontage to satisfy zoning; septic lots are subject to Groundwater Protection review. Flooding/standing-water/seasonally high-water-table areas may be excluded from minimum lot area, and lots without public water must be at least 25,000 sq ft. These rules make floodplain and septic mapping decisive before assigning a lot yield.

### Provisional development scenarios

1. **One premium creek/wooded homesite:** most defensible fallback if a dry elevated homesite/perk area exists. Lowest infrastructure exposure.
2. **2–4 larger homesites:** plausible only if the parcel has multiple independent dry/perk envelopes and compliant frontage/access. This is the capital-efficient subdivision scenario worth testing first.
3. **Higher-density small lots / internal road:** currently weak. Narrow access, creek/flood constraints, septic uncertainty and infrastructure burden could erase the theoretical density advantage.
4. **Build one modest home and sell:** viable fallback only after confirming flood-safe homesite, septic and economical utilities. Nearby conventional Hixson homes commonly transact in roughly the $300k–$400k range, but land basis at $225k leaves limited room for a speculative build unless the site supports a premium product or residual land can be sold separately.
