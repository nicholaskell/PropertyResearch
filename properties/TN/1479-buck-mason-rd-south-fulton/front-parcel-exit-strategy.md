# Front Parcel Resale / Build-Back Strategy

Research date: 2026-09-11

## Strategy

Potential plan: acquire the full 53.5-acre property, occupy the existing 2-bed/2-bath house while constructing a new permanent residence in the northern portion of the long interior field, then divide and sell the existing house together with all current outbuildings and a small acreage tract along Buck Mason Road. Retain the large rear/interior acreage as the permanent homestead.

User-supplied aerial imagery shows the existing house/outbuilding cluster concentrated near the eastern Buck Mason Road frontage, while a large majority of the tract extends west/northwest behind it. This visually supports the strategy, but the screenshot boundary is not a survey.

## Preliminary Conclusion

**Strategy rating: STRONG / worth designing before purchase.**

The parcel configuration appears unusually compatible with a front improved-parcel carve-out because the existing improvements are concentrated near the road, the parent parcel reportedly has >1,300 ft of road frontage, and the retained tract should be capable of preserving substantial acreage and potentially independent Buck Mason Road access. The critical implementation rule is to design the future division, driveway, utilities, septic sites and lender partial-release mechanics before locating the new house.

## Best Economic Split

**Preliminary preference: approximately 5–7.5 acres with the existing house/building package.**

Why:
- Five acres is enough to market the property as a genuine hobby-farm/country acreage rather than a conventional house lot.
- TDEC's septic-program guidance treats divisions where all resulting tracts are 5+ acres favorably for its subdivision definition, although other plat/planning rules can still apply.
- The buyer receives the complete existing improvement cluster, public water, septic and fiber.
- Nicholas preserves ~46–48.5 acres instead of surrendering 10 acres for what may be only ~$20k–$35k of incremental resale proceeds over the 5-acre configuration.
- Preserving additional acreage creates more value/options for future child homesites, shop/business separation, woods/pasture and privacy.

**Current sweet spot: target a survey concept around 5.0–6.5 acres**, then enlarge only if the improvement layout, septic field, frontage geometry or buyer-market evidence makes additional land materially more valuable.

## Site-Planning Rules Before New Construction

1. **Survey the intended front carve-out before choosing the new house pad.** Preserve independent road frontage/access for the retained tract rather than relying on an easement through the sold front parcel if avoidable.
2. **Locate and map the existing septic tank, field and reserve/repair area.** All of it should remain wholly inside the front resale parcel with adequate setbacks.
3. **Map public-water service and meter.** Design the new house with an independently serviceable meter/line; do not create a private cross-parcel utility dependency.
4. **Confirm fiber architecture.** The retained parcel/new house should receive its own serviceable drop/address rather than depending on facilities attached to the sold house.
5. **Establish new driveway/culvert access early.** The retained parcel should function independently before the front parcel is sold.
6. **Keep the new house visually/private separated.** The northern long-field concept appears promising because it avoids placing the new residence immediately behind the sold house, but flood/soil/septic mapping must determine the actual pad.
7. **Do not give away unnecessary Buck Mason frontage.** Shape the front parcel around the improvement cluster while retaining enough road frontage for the rear tract and possible future child tracts.
8. **Greenbelt planning:** obtain assessor scenario calculations before recording a split so remaining qualifying acreage stays in Greenbelt where possible and rollback liability is understood.
9. **Mortgage partial release:** if the purchase loan encumbers the entire 53.5 acres, obtain lender rules for a future partial release before closing on the original acquisition. A lender may require appraisal, minimum retained collateral/LTV and application of some or all sale proceeds to principal.
10. **Title/deed:** confirm there are no restrictions/easements that interfere with division or separate residential use.

## Financial Interpretation

The strategy improves the property's exit profile because the current house can serve as temporary housing during construction and later become a separately marketable rural asset. The likely economic optimum is not maximizing the sale price of the front parcel; it is maximizing **sale proceeds per acre surrendered** while leaving the retained homestead with the best frontage, access, privacy and future optionality.

On current evidence, **5–7.5 acres appears superior to 10 acres**. A 10-acre sale parcel could fetch more, but the likely extra proceeds may not compensate Nicholas for permanently giving up another 2.5–5 acres of strategically useful land.
