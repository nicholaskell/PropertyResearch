## Access — decisive issue

Montgomery County's current road list confirms **Southside Rd itself** is a county-maintained hot-mix road, 9.71 miles long, with 50-ft ROW and 20-ft roadway. That does **not** prove the subject touches maintained Southside Rd. The listing aerial shows the tract behind other parcels and the listing explicitly references an old county road.

County zoning §4.3 states no building may be erected unless access is via a legally qualifying public street, approved subdivision street, or conforming permanent easement; building lots generally need at least 25 ft of qualifying usable frontage unless the zoning district requires more. Current subdivision regulations can also require widening/improvement of inadequate roads used by a development.

**Before treating this as a homesite, obtain:**
1. Both parcel IDs and recorded deeds/plats.
2. Name/ROW width/legal status of the old county road and where county maintenance currently ends.
3. Written County Highway + RPC determination whether one house can receive a permit today and what road work is required.
4. Engineer/contractor budget for clearing, grading, drainage/culverts, base and paving if owner-funded improvements are required.
5. Whether subdivision would trigger a major plat/public-infrastructure requirement.

County Highway: 931-648-5740. County requires permission before work in county highway ROW and instructs applicants to call Highway first for county-road driveway permits.

## Development / subdivision

The listing's subdivision claim is **plausible but not yet bankable**. RPC defines a major plat as 2+ lots requiring public infrastructure improvements or 6+ lots without them; minor plats cover 2–5 lots without public infrastructure. Because this tract apparently depends on an unimproved/old road, subdivision could move directly into a more expensive infrastructure process.

At 43.3 acres, the gross acreage is sufficient for multiple lots, but **do not value hypothetical lots yet**. Road construction, frontage, septic suitability, zoning, topography, drainage and utility extensions determine the real yield.

**Development Optionality: PLAUSIBLE, potentially STRONG if road/access is cheaply solvable.**
