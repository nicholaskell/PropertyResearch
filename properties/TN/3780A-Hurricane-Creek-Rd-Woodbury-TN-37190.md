# Property Summary

3780A Hurricane Creek Rd is an approximately 94-acre farm/woodland offering in Cannon County near Woodbury, Tennessee, with a small existing farmhouse. It remains a serious portfolio contender because of acreage, privacy, very low property taxes, wood/homestead/business utility and proximity to Middle Tennessee demand. The central uncertainty is not nominal acreage but how much of the offered 94 acres is genuinely useful after steep terrain, creek/flood constraints and wooded slopes—and whether the advertised 94-acre conveyance is already legally separated from the assessor's 107-acre parcel.

## 2026-09-09 Backlog Research — Material Findings

### P0 — Acreage / legal conveyance — PARTIALLY RESOLVED
Current RealTracs-derived listing evidence explicitly says the approximately **13-acre tract across the creek and across the road from the farmhouse is NOT included** in the $499,900 offering. The earlier MLS/assessor-derived record for parcel **013 01200 000** reports **107 acres**; the current offering reports **94 acres calculated from plat**. The arithmetic matches the excluded tract almost exactly (107 - 13 = 94), but public listing evidence does not establish that the 94 acres has already been legally subdivided into a separately conveyable parcel.

**Implication:** Do not underwrite this as a clean 94-acre parcel until the current survey/plat and deed legal description are obtained. If the seller is carving 94 acres from a 107-acre tax parcel at closing, subdivision/recording, access, tax allocation and legal-description details must be resolved before commitment.

**Status:** PARTIALLY RESOLVED.  
**Required external action:** obtain the current plat/survey and proposed deed legal description/title commitment from the listing agent/title company; confirm the exact 94 acres, frontage, creek/road split, excluded 13-acre tract and whether any new parcel approval/recording is required.

### P0 — Farmhouse as interim housing — PARTIALLY RESOLVED / HIGHER RISK
Current listing sources describe the 1,229-sq-ft, 2-bed/1-bath circa-1900 structure, but MLS-derived fields report **no heating, no cooling, no appliances**, while the earlier farm listing for the assessor parcel classified **Habitable Residence: No**. The current listing is marketed as a single-family residence, so these fields may reflect incomplete MLS data rather than a legal condemnation or actual uninhabitability. They are nevertheless direct evidence against assuming the farmhouse is immediately comfortable move-in housing.

**Implication:** The prior strategy of living in the farmhouse while building is now a decision-critical condition, not a safe assumption. This finding can materially reduce Brittany fit and increase effective cost-to-goal.

**Status:** PARTIALLY RESOLVED.  
**Required external action:** seller property disclosure plus in-person/inspection verification of heat source, cooling, electrical, plumbing/hot water, septic function, water connection, roof/foundation and whether the dwelling currently has legal occupancy/habitability status.

### P0 — Flood / creek / terrain — OPEN
The listing consistently describes **hilly, level, sloped and wooded** terrain and creek frontage. A third-party climate model flags high building flood exposure, but that is not authoritative FEMA evidence and is not sufficient to resolve the task. Parcel-level FEMA/NWI/topographic overlay is still required, particularly because the farmhouse and road/creek relationship may constrain the most convenient operational ground.
