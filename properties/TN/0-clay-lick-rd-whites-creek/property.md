## Regulatory Deep Dive — 2026-09-17

### AR2a base zoning — CONFIRMED
Metro describes AR2a as agricultural/residential zoning with a nominal 2-acre minimum lot size. It permits rural residential/agricultural uses and is intended for areas under Natural Conservation or Interim Non-Urban policy. Current Metro/Planning precedents identify the following AR2a bulk standards: 2-acre minimum base lot size, 20% maximum building coverage, 40-foot minimum street setback, 20-foot side setback, 20-foot rear setback, and 3-story maximum height.

Single-family residential is permitted. Two-family residential is permitted with conditions. Mobile homes are among the uses contemplated by AR2a, but this does not mean RV occupancy is permitted.

### Subdivision — PLAUSIBLE BUT NOT “11.36 / 2 = 5 LOTS”
The zoning arithmetic gives a theoretical ceiling of five 2-acre lots, but Metro's Rural Character Subdivision Regulations can impose substantially larger/contextual lots, frontage/depth rules, conservation areas, building envelopes, shared access, and environmental constraints.

Metro defines a subdivision as division into two or more parcels where resulting parcels are under five acres; such division requires subdivision review/platting. Chapter 4 Rural Character Subdivision standards apply in T2/rural policy areas. The Countryside Character option can require contextual lot sizes based on surrounding parcels rather than simply the AR2a 2-acre floor.

Important local precedent: in an August 2024 AR2a subdivision on Clay Lick Road, Metro reviewed two lots of about 3.40 and 4.35 acres. The staff report states Clay Lick Road is a collector and therefore required a shared-access easement. That same report confirms 40-foot street, 20-foot side/rear setbacks and 20% building coverage for AR2a.

### Rural Character / conservation constraints — MATERIAL
For rural-character subdivisions, Metro requires identification of Primary Conservation Land before design when new streets or joint-access easements are proposed. The remaining land becomes the development footprint. Current Chapter 4 language is restrictive on slopes: grading is not permitted in areas over 20% slope under the cited rural-character provision; certain 15–20% slope areas can require exceptions and critical-lot plans. Building envelopes cannot be placed in protected/conservation areas.

The listing explicitly calls this parcel hilly and claims a year-round creek, so these are not abstract risks. A slope map and hydrologic determination are gating items for development yield.

### Creek / stormwater — MATERIAL
Metro requires water-quality buffers along community waters including streams, rivers, springs, lakes, ponds and wetlands for new development/significant redevelopment and sites requiring grading permits. Buffer impacts are controlled by the Stormwater Management Manual and generally must be minimized; unauthorized disturbance can require restoration. Floodway development is generally prohibited and floodplain fill has compensatory-cut requirements.

The listing's “year round creek” should be treated as a potential development constraint until Metro Water Services/FEMA mapping and a hydrologic determination establish the channel classification, floodway/floodplain and exact buffer.

### RV / camper occupancy — NOT CONFIRMED; TREAT AS A RED FLAG

Metro defines recreational vehicles as temporary living quarters designed primarily for recreational/camping/travel/seasonal use, not permanent dwelling use. AR2a's allowance for mobile homes is not the same thing as an RV/travel trailer.

Therefore construction-period RV occupancy must remain UNKNOWN / INVESTIGATE, not assumed legal. Before purchase, obtain written confirmation from Metro Codes/Zoning addressing: whether an owner may occupy one RV on vacant AR2a land while a permitted residence is actively under construction; whether a building permit must first be issued; maximum duration/renewals; required septic/wastewater, potable water and electric connections; address/911 requirements; and whether the RV must be removed/vacated at certificate of occupancy.

Parking/storing an RV and occupying it as a dwelling are separate questions.

### Shop / detached accessory structure — POSSIBLE, BUT RESIDENTIAL USE RESTRICTED
Metro requires permits for accessory structures such as garages/sheds over applicable thresholds. Detached accessory structures with plumbing/HVAC/electrical trades require a recorded restrictive covenant. Metro states electrical service up to 100 amps can be allowed after the covenant; plumbing is generally limited to a toilet and hand sink, and showers/bathing facilities are generally not permitted in a standard detached accessory structure. This materially undermines a “build shop first, put full bathroom in it, and live in RV/shop” plan unless separately approved as a lawful dwelling/use.

### DADU / second dwelling — DO NOT ASSUME
Metro's DADU rules require a principal single-family structure and restrict DADUs geographically to the Urban Services District, a DADU overlay in the General Services District, or an applicable Specific Plan. The nearby 2910 Claylick Rd public record identifies this stretch as General Services District. No DADU overlay or SP has yet been confirmed for the subject parcel. Treat detached accessory dwelling eligibility as UNKNOWN and likely unavailable absent an overlay/SP confirmation.

AR2a may allow a two-family use with conditions, which is a separate path from a DADU and needs site-specific zoning review.

### Chickens / farm animals — GENERALLY FAVORABLE
AR2a is agricultural zoning. Metro's chicken ordinance history expressly notes that farm animals may be kept on AG/AR2a property and that the residential hen permit requirement does not apply to AG/AR2a parcels. Exact commercial livestock/kennel/business operations can trigger additional rules, so scale and business use still need separate review.

### Home business / commercial use — LIMITED
Metro permits home occupations subject to operational limits, including residential character, customer/employee/vehicle and nuisance restrictions. A serious firewood/sawmill or contractor-yard operation should not be assumed permitted merely because the parcel is AR2a; the exact use classification and conditions need Codes/Planning confirmation.
