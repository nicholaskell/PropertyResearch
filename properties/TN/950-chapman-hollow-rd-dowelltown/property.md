## Zoning / Building / Business Freedom

DeKalb County government states that **unincorporated DeKalb County has no zoning ordinances** and no county permitting office; the county adopted Tennessee state building codes. This is unusually favorable for accessory buildings, equipment, homestead uses and potentially firewood/sawmill activity, subject to confirmation that the parcel is outside municipal limits and compliance with state building, septic, environmental, nuisance, road-access and any private deed/easement restrictions.

For Nicholas/Kell & Son, lack of county zoning is a material positive, but it does not automatically authorize a noisy commercial sawmill, retail traffic, employees, signage, wastewater/discharges or structures without applicable state/code approvals. Confirm business licensing, nuisance exposure, driveway/road suitability and any deed restrictions before operating commercially.

At $285k (~$5,018/acre), the basis is much more defensible, but fair value depends heavily on usable acreage, access, septic sites, timber quality and broadband.

### Benchmark comparison — De Pere / Brown County

This is a trade from a stronger, deeper suburban/exurban housing market into a **growing but materially thinner rural-land market**. De Pere remains superior for immediate resale liquidity and established development pressure. Chapman Hollow offers dramatically lower taxes, much more land/privacy and greater apparent use freedom. Its appreciation case rests on Middle Tennessee/Nashville-region spillover, Center Hill Lake/rural-recreation demand and scarce usable acreage—not on De Pere-style suburban scarcity.
