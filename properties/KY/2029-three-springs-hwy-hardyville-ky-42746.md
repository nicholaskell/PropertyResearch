## Land Reality / Karst

102 total acres; MLS claims ~80 productive cropland acres. Pond, rear creek, rolling terrain and partial barbed-wire fencing. If the cropland claim verifies, usable/prime acreage is excellent.

**Karst is now a first-tier land due-diligence item.** Kentucky Geological Survey reports **15,876 mapped sinkholes in Hart County**, and a 2026 study using 2014/2023 lidar identified **2,633 cover-collapse sinkholes that formed countywide during that period**. The occurrence rate is strongly geology-dependent and must not be applied mechanically to this parcel, but Hart County is demonstrably an unusually active karst environment. A parcel-specific KGS sinkhole/bedrock overlay, lidar review and geotechnical evaluation of any new homesite/shop area are warranted.

The existence of productive cropland does not eliminate sinkhole risk; depressions can be farmed while still constraining buildings, septic systems, ponds, roads or heavy structures.

## Zoning, Second Dwelling & Development

Hart County Planning Commission administers subdivision regulations. Important findings from the county rules:

- New subdivision lots generally require at least **75 feet of frontage** on a qualifying road.
- New divisions on state/federal highways require an approved highway entrance before plat approval.
- New lots are subject to a **maximum depth-to-width ratio of 4:1**, including tracts larger than 10 acres; flag-lot access strips generally should not exceed 500 feet.
- **Only one principal building is permitted on an individual lot** except in specified planned-development structures. This means Thomas's future independent house is not something to assume can simply be placed elsewhere on the existing parcel; a compliant lot split or another approved development structure is likely necessary.
- The agricultural-use exemption requires at least five contiguous acres **and genuine agricultural use**; it is not a shortcut for residential development. The county form specifically says an agricultural division is not for residential development for sale/lease and future non-agricultural development remains restricted until subdivision rules are satisfied.

The rules also contain standards for commercial/manufacturing parking and truck loading where no local zoning ordinance is in effect, which is encouraging for business optionality, but this does **not** yet prove unrestricted commercial sawmilling or firewood sales at the subject property. Written county confirmation remains required.

## Pipeline / Power-Line Deep Dive

The listing's **Gas Pipeline, Power Lines** disclosure appears deliberate rather than generic. Hart County subdivision regulations specifically address **high-pressure pipeline and high-tension power-line easements**, requiring development to minimize encroachment, regulating road crossings and generally keeping septic systems on the same side of the easement as the served building unless written waivers are obtained.

PHMSA/Kentucky PSC/Kinder Morgan sources confirm that **Tennessee Gas Pipeline Company (Kinder Morgan)** operates interstate gas-transmission facilities in Hart County, including the **Monroe SMS** delivery point. Kinder Morgan's current TGP maps show the Zone 2 system running through Hart County. This makes TGP a **strong operator candidate**, but I have not yet established that the line crossing the 102-acre tract is TGP; NPMS public mapping is intentionally generalized and has stated spatial accuracy limitations.

Practical consequence: even if the pipeline only consumes a narrow strip, it can materially affect subdivision geometry, shop/homesite placement, septic placement, fencing/trees, road crossings and future buyer perception. We need the recorded easement—not merely a pipeline map.
