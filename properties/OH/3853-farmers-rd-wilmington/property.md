## Jurisdiction and subdivision path

The listing legal description places the tract principally in Washington Township, with about 0.105 acre in Green Township. Washington Township has been reported as one of Clinton County's two unzoned townships. Therefore there is likely no township residential zoning district setting a minimum lot size. This does **not** mean unrestricted development.

Clinton County Regional Planning Commission administers county subdivision regulations. A frontage-only division can potentially use the minor-lot-split process if it satisfies Ohio Revised Code 711.131 and county rules: existing public street frontage, no new street, adequate access, health approval and the applicable limit on lots/divisions. Five or more additional lots, a new road, or a plan that falls outside minor-split criteria can trigger a major subdivision/plat process.

### Most credible concept

- Retained corner/homestead parcel: roughly 3–4 acres.
- Three sale lots: roughly 2.5–3 acres each, each fronting an existing public road.
- Avoid an internal road, shared drive and centralized utilities.
- Treat four total homesites as the current base-case ceiling until survey, Health and Engineer staff confirm otherwise.
- A fifth homesite is theoretical and should not be underwritten.

### Required approvals / evidence

1. Recorded boundary survey and legal description for the 12.2 acres.
2. Written pre-application determination from CCRPC identifying minor versus major subdivision and allowable lot count.
3. Road classification, ROW, frontage, driveway spacing and sight-distance review from the Clinton County Engineer or relevant road authority.
4. Soil/site evaluation for **every proposed lot**, including reserve replacement area.
5. Well feasibility and isolation-distance layout.
6. Title commitment, deed chain, easement instrument, crop lease termination/possession terms and mineral exception schedule.
7. FEMA/NWI/GIS overlay against surveyed boundary.
8. Spectrum plant map and written construction quote for each planned address.
9. New-parcel tax estimate from Auditor.

## Septic and land constraints

Clinton County Health District requires a site and soil evaluation before residential sewage-treatment design. The most important diligence step is not one generic perc test; it is a conceptual lot plan followed by separate evaluations on each proposed homesite, with adequate area for the primary system and replacement area. Cropland may be productive yet seasonally wet, tiled, slowly permeable or limited by a shallow seasonal water table. Expect engineered/mounded or other higher-cost systems if conventional gravity systems are not supported.

Provisional land model:

- Gross: 12.2 acres.
- Road ROW/corners/drainage/utility and layout loss: about 1.0–2.0 acres.
- Provisionally usable: about 10–11 acres.
- Prime operational land after four independent home/septic envelopes: about 8–10 acres.
- Confidence: low until survey and soil evaluations.

## RV occupancy

Because Washington Township is unzoned, a township zoning prohibition may not apply. However, no authoritative rule found in this pass affirmatively authorizes living in an RV on vacant land during construction. Treat this as **not cleared**.

Ask Clinton County Building & Zoning and Health in writing:

- May an owner occupy one RV on an unzoned Washington Township parcel while constructing a permitted single-family dwelling?
- Is an issued building permit and active inspections schedule required?
- Maximum duration and renewal?
- Must the RV connect to an approved septic system, or may a sealed holding tank/pump contract be used?
- Are well/potable water, electric inspection, 911 address and driveway approval prerequisites?
- Does the answer change after a lot split or when more than one dwelling is planned?

Practical setup should include a gravel pad, drainage, frost-protected water, legal wastewater, 50-amp power and all-weather construction access.
