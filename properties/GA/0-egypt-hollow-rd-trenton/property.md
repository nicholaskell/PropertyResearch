## Regulatory implications
Dade County is materially more permissive than a conventional zoned county for residential land use. The practical controlling layers for this project appear to be subdivision approval, septic/health approval, 911/access, electrical inspection, flood rules, driveway/right-of-way requirements and erosion/sedimentation regulation rather than residential zoning density. This is a meaningful positive for micro-homestead or rural-lot concepts, but it does not make steep or non-percing land developable.

The county permit page specifically identifies Land Subdivision, Driveway Pipe Installation, County Road Acceptance, Flood Damage Prevention, Soil Erosion and Sedimentation Control, Emergency 911 Address and Utility Accommodations as regulated areas. Exact subdivision design standards are not reliably surfaced online and require direct confirmation with the county commission/public works office.

## Septic / lot-size implications
Georgia DPH's current onsite manual recommends 43,560 sq ft minimum and 150 ft width for single-family lots with an individual water supply, while warning that larger lots may be required for slope over 5%, poor soils, replacement-field requirements, drainage and adverse topography. On this parcel, septic suitability is likely to dictate practical lot yield more than nominal acreage.

For underwriting, do not assume 1-acre or 2-acre lots solely because the acreage math works. A viable created lot needs: buildable pad, access/driveway, primary septic field, replacement field, well separation, drainage and enough geometry to avoid severe grading.
