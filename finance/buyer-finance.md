# Buyer Financial Profile

This file contains the buyer's current financial assumptions for property comparisons. Keep it separate from property facts and personas.

## Current Housing Baseline

- Estimated current mortgage balance: **~$225,000**
- Estimated current monthly PITI (principal, interest, taxes, insurance): **~$1,600/month**

Use this as the baseline for candidate affordability comparisons. For every serious contender, show:

- Estimated candidate PITI/monthly property carry
- Dollar change versus current ~$1,600/month
- Percentage change versus current housing cost when useful

Do not assume the current mortgage balance is the exact payoff amount; use a current payoff statement when available.

## Capital Priorities

1. Preserve the minimum cash reserve.
2. Use enough down payment to create an acceptable payment/financing structure.
3. Preserve capital for high-value improvements when that creates more utility/value than additional down payment.
4. Do not assume every dollar of home-sale proceeds should be used as down payment.
5. Compare buying a feature already built versus buying cheaper and adding it later.

## Home Sale Proceeds

Use three scenarios until actual net proceeds are known:

- **Conservative:** lower-end net proceeds after mortgage payoff, selling costs, taxes/fees and known obligations.
- **Expected:** most likely net proceeds.
- **Optimistic:** reasonable high-end proceeds, not a best-case fantasy.

When estimating sale proceeds before listing/closing, begin with an evidence-based sale-price range, subtract the estimated/current mortgage payoff (~$225,000 until updated), estimated selling costs, concessions/credits and other applicable closing obligations. Clearly distinguish **gross equity** from **net spendable proceeds**.

Once the current home closes, replace scenarios with actual available capital while preserving historical scenarios if useful.

## Down Payment / Cash Allocation

For serious contenders, evaluate multiple structures where useful:

- Minimum practical down payment
- Preferred/balanced down payment
- Larger down payment
- Retain cash for immediate improvements
- Retain cash for reserves/flexibility

Do not recommend a larger down payment merely because cash exists. Compare the return in monthly-payment reduction against the value created by improvements or retained liquidity.

## Monthly Carry

Use current financing assumptions/rates when analyzing a live purchase. Estimate:

- Principal + interest
- Property tax
- Homeowners insurance
- PMI when applicable
- HOA/required fees
- Other material recurring property carrying costs when known

Clearly label estimates and rate assumptions. Do not hard-code mortgage rates in this repository.

For every serious candidate, include:

`Estimated candidate monthly carry: $X | $Y more/less than current ~$1,600/month`

This comparison should be shown even when the candidate is otherwise affordable, because it captures the lifestyle/cash-flow trade being made.

## Equity / Future Financing

Track equity created by the down payment separately from cash retained after closing. Future borrowing against equity may be evaluated as an option for shops, barns, renovations, second-homesite infrastructure or other improvements, but should never be assumed available or advisable without considering rates, lending rules, risk and cash flow.

## Financial Fit

Financial Fit is derived, not a persona. Consider:

- Monthly payment versus target/max
- Monthly change versus current housing baseline
- Cash required at closing
- Cash/reserve remaining
- Immediate capital needs
- Effective property cost
- Taxes/insurance/carrying cost
- Cost to reach desired functionality
- Financing flexibility
- Value/equity cushion

## Key Metrics

**Effective Property Cost = Purchase Price + Required Improvements + Desired Improvements**

**Cash to Goal = Cash required before the property reaches the selected acceptable/desired state**

**Housing Cost Delta = Estimated Candidate Monthly Carry - Current Monthly PITI (~$1,600 until updated)**

Cash to Goal is not the same as Effective Property Cost because financed purchase dollars and retained/required cash affect the buyer differently.
