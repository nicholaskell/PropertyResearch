- **Harry Cherry** remains the strongest full-vision property because it combines 115.3 acres, an existing habitable base, exact-address gigabit fiber, extensive frontage, AG zoning and a credible live-build-split-resell path in a growing market.

### Properties that move down

- **Buck Mason, Whitney Branch, Waverly and the U.P. properties** remain excellent land/homestead candidates but lose relative rank because their routine dating pools and resale buyer depth are weaker.
- **Large remote PA tracts** lose points for OGM/access/title complexity, buildout cost and weaker dating convenience even when land and firewood potential are excellent.
- **High-priced Fox Valley raw land** retains excellent resale/development scores but is less attractive for the move itself: the purchase basis plus house/shop construction consumes too much capital while failing to improve climate.
- **1156 Langbrae Dr** gains dating/location points but remains a poor land-dream value: $435,000 buys only five sloped wooded acres, two bedrooms and no detached shop.
- **3500 E Lemon Rd** falls under the landing strategy because the cabin is not established as a legal permanent residence, no conventional septic/grid power is documented, and Jefferson County's RV rules are unfavorable.
- **N1846 Harbor Rd, Peshtigo** remains a reject because landfill/buildability/environmental issues overwhelm its cheap price.
